The Problem

India's Groundwater Crisis

A crisis affecting 600 million people that demands data-driven intervention

⚠️ Why This Matters

India is the world's largest consumer of groundwater, with 85% of rural drinking water and over 60% of agriculture depending entirely on it. According to NITI Aayog, nearly 600 million people face high-to-extreme water stress. National water demand is projected to be twice the available supply by 2030.


MGNREGA, one of the world's largest public employment programs, allocates substantial resources for building water conservation structures — but studies show that 62% of flood protection works suffer partial damage, and up to 76% of drought-proofing assets are fully damaged. The problem isn't budget or will — it's intelligent siting and planning.

Our Approach

Decoupled Two-Model Framework

Separating social vulnerability identification from hydrological siting

🏘️

Model 1 — Priority Map

A social vulnerability model that uses CNNs to analyse multi-spectral satellite imagery (nighttime lights, land use, settlement patterns) and predict high-resolution poverty and water-need indices. Bridges the critical data gap left by the outdated 2011 Census by training on (2011 Satellite + Terrain → 2011 Census) and then predicting on current imagery.

💧

Model 2 — Siting Model

A hybrid learning architecture for hydrological siting. Uses a CNN (ResNet18) for deep feature extraction from 6-band geospatial data, producing 512-dimensional embeddings that capture complex spatial patterns. These are combined with tabular features and fed into XGBoost for robust binary classification of site suitability.


🌐 CoRE Stack Aligned
Data Platform

CoRE Stack Integration

Leveraging India's geospatial data platform for comprehensive analysis

CoRE Stack (Computing Resource for Earth) provides the geospatial layers and datasets that power this pipeline. The siting model draws upon hydrological layers including net water balance, aquifer type, and groundwater extraction stages; climate indicators such as drought intensity and frequency; land use and land cover classification; and terrain classification data. These layers ensure that the model's predictions are grounded in real, physically meaningful environmental data rather than arbitrary proxies.
